This policy explains what the mobile game Ashborne ("the game") does with information about you.

1. Who we are

Ashborne is made by Eduardo Pahl (Pahl Labs on Google Play), an independent developer in Brazil who publishes as CatByte Labs ("we", "us"). He is the controller of the personal data described here, and he is also our data protection contact (the encarregado under Brazil's LGPD).

  • E-mail: incpahl@gmail.com
  • The current version of this policy is always at https://eduardopahl.github.io/privacy/ashborne/. The game links to it in Settings → Privacy.

2. The short version

  • The game needs no account and never asks for your name, e-mail or phone number.
  • Your progress is saved on your device.
  • Every video is optional. There are two, and each starts only when you tap it:
    • one to come back once in a run;
    • one to double the Ember of a run.
    They are shown by Unity's ad services (Unity LevelPlay and Unity Ads), which receive information about your device, its IP address, its advertising ID and how you interact with the ad. Personalised ads are off unless you turn them on.
  • Statistics and crash reports are off unless you turn them on.
  • Purchases go through Google Play. We never see your card or payment details.
  • If you are signed in to Google Play Games, your best times go to the leaderboards.
  • We do not sell your personal data.

3. What stays on your device

The game keeps a save file with:

  • your progress: Ember, Enchantments, unlocked heroes and maps, records, Deeds and Mastery;
  • your settings;
  • your answers on the privacy screen;
  • whether you own the Supporter Pack, so it works offline.

The file lives in the game's private storage, is signed against tampering, and is never uploaded by us.

Android backup. If backup is on for your device, Android may copy the game's data to your Google account, under Google's terms, and bring it back when you set up a new phone. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its data, deletes the copy on the device. A backup copy stays in your Google account until you delete it there or Google lets it expire.

4. Your choices

On first launch, the game shows a privacy screen with two switches. Both are off until you turn them on:

SwitchWhat it allows
Statistics & crash reportsanonymous run statistics and crash reports (section 6)
Personalised adsthe ad network may use your data to choose ads for you (section 5)

You can change both at any time in Settings → Privacy, and the game is the same either way.

Apart from the engine information in section 6.2, nothing leaves your device before you answer this screen. That screen is also where you give or refuse consent to personalised ads, everywhere, including the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland: personalised ads stay off unless you turn them on. Your answer is passed to the ad services through Unity's consent interface, and you can change it at any time in Settings → Privacy.

5. The optional videos (ads)

The game offers rewarded videos in two places and nowhere else:

  • after you fall, once per run, if the run passed three minutes;
  • at the end of a run, to double the Ember earned.

There are no banners and no ads between runs. You never have to watch.

The videos are served by Unity Technologies: Unity LevelPlay (the ad service in the game) and Unity Ads (the network that supplies the videos). They start when you have answered the privacy screen. When you load or watch a video, they process:

  • your device's IP address, from which they infer an approximate location (country, region, city);
  • device identifiers, including the Android advertising ID and an app installation ID;
  • device and app information: model, operating system, language, screen, app version, and technical diagnostics;
  • your interaction with the ad: loading, viewing, skipping, tapping;
  • in-app purchase events, which Unity Ads uses for analytics and advertising.

Why: to deliver the video and give you the reward, to count views, to cap how often an ad repeats, to measure the ad and to prevent fraud.

Personalised ads. Unity uses this information to choose ads based on your interests only if you turn on "Personalised ads". Otherwise the ads are contextual: chosen from the game and the moment, not from a profile of you. The game passes your choice to Unity every time it starts:

  • Unity's consent setting;
  • in the US, a "do not sell or share" signal when personalised ads are off;
  • in every case, a signal that the game is not directed to children.

Your controls inside the ad. Every Unity ad has a Data Privacy icon. It lets you see the data Unity holds for your device in this game, opt out of personalised ads and ask Unity to delete it.

Unity is an independent controller of this data, under the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy. Unity's privacy contact is DPO@unity3d.com. We do not receive this data.

If you own the Supporter Pack, you get the same rewards with a tap and no video is shown.

6. Statistics, crash reports and engine data

6.1 Only if you turn on "Statistics & crash reports"

With this switch on, the game may send to Unity Technologies (Unity Analytics and Unity Diagnostics), acting on our behalf, without your name or advertising ID:

  • run events: when a run starts and ends — the hero, the map, how long it lasted, the level and cycle reached, how it ended, the number of Vows, whether you came back by video, whether you doubled the Ember;
  • first-session steps: whether you finished the first launch and the first run, started a second one and bought your first Enchantment, each once;
  • crash reports: device model, operating system, game version, the error, and the last lines the game logged about what it was doing;
  • a random installation ID and session times, which is how returning players are counted.

Why: to find crashes, and to see where the game is too hard, too easy or confusing.

Kept: statistics for up to 13 months, crash reports for up to 90 days, then deleted by Unity. If you turn the switch off, collection stops at once and we ask Unity to delete your installation's statistics, which Unity does within 30 days.

6.2 Engine data

Ashborne is built with the Unity engine. In the version we use, it sends Unity Technologies, on every launch and whatever you choose:

  • anonymous hardware statistics: device model, operating system, processor, graphics chip, memory and screen;
  • engine diagnostics: engine and app version, and engine errors.

Unity uses them to support and improve the engine. They do not include your name, your advertising ID or your progress, and we cannot switch them off in our version of the engine.

7. Purchases

The Supporter Pack (one-time) and the tip (optional, as many times as you like) are sold through Google Play, using Unity's purchasing library (Unity IAP) on top of Google Play Billing.

  • Google handles the payment under the Google Privacy Policy. We never see your card, bank or payment details.
  • The game only learns what was bought and whether the payment is confirmed. It keeps that on your device, and checks the receipt on the device as well.
  • Unity's purchasing library may send Unity short-lived technical diagnostics so the purchase flow works.

8. Leaderboards (Google Play Games)

If you are signed in to Google Play Games on your device, the game signs in automatically after you have answered the privacy screen. It then sends Google your best time for each hero on each map: 24 leaderboards. Google adds your Play Games profile (gamertag, avatar and player ID).

  • A run you gave up is never sent, and neither is a run played with Vows.
  • If you came back by video, the time sent is the moment of your first fall, exactly as if you had not watched.
  • Who sees your entry depends on your Play Games profile visibility (everyone, friends or only you).
  • Google also collects its own diagnostics to keep the service stable. Everything here happens under the Google Privacy Policy.

To stop:

  • turn off automatic sign-in to games in the Play Games settings;
  • or delete this game's data from your Play Games profile. That also removes your leaderboard entries.

6.1 Só se você ligar "Estatísticas e relatórios de erro"

Com essa chave ligada, o jogo pode enviar à Unity Technologies (Unity Analytics e Unity Diagnostics), que trata os dados em nosso nome, sem o seu nome e sem o ID de publicidade:

  • eventos de partida: quando uma partida começa e termina — o herói, o mapa, quanto durou, o nível e o ciclo alcançados, como terminou, o número de Votos, se você voltou por vídeo, se dobrou a Ember;
  • passos da primeira sessão: se você concluiu a primeira abertura e a primeira partida, começou uma segunda e comprou o primeiro Encantamento, cada um uma vez;
  • relatórios de erro: modelo do aparelho, sistema, versão do jogo, o erro e as últimas linhas que o jogo registrou sobre o que fazia;
  • um ID aleatório da instalação e o horário das sessões, que é como se contam os jogadores que voltam.

Para quê: achar travamentos e ver onde o jogo está difícil demais, fácil demais ou confuso.

Guardados: estatísticas por até 13 meses e relatórios de erro por até 90 dias, e depois apagados pela Unity. Se você desligar a chave, a coleta para na hora e pedimos à Unity que apague as estatísticas da sua instalação, o que ela faz em até 30 dias.

A Unity usa esses dados para dar suporte ao motor e melhorá-lo. Eles não incluem o seu nome, o seu ID de publicidade nem o seu progresso, e não temos como desligá-los nesta versão do motor.

16. Segurança

O arquivo de save é assinado e fica no armazenamento privado do jogo. Tudo o que o jogo e os parceiros enviam trafega criptografado (HTTPS). Não temos servidor nem banco de dados próprios.

17. Mudanças
